Odliadur

Welsh end-rhyme finder. Type an ending to see the words that share it.

The book's 560 chapter endings, in Welsh alphabetical order. Use the arrows to move along; searching centres the list on your ending.

Welsh has two different wy sounds that do not rhyme with each other (lleddf, as in mwyn, and talgron, as in gwyn). This version goes by spelling only. It keeps -wy apart from -y, but it cannot tell which sound a particular word has: some words listed here are talgron, as the book files under -y (for example compounds of gwŷdd such as tanwydd).

    Word list: the Welsh spelling dictionary from Bangor University's Language Technologies Unit (Uned Technolegau Iaith) and Troi, licensed under the GNU LGPL v3 or later. Source: techiaith/hunspell-cy. Plural forms were added by adding the regular endings that dictionary defines.

    Meanings: most come from Wiktionary (Welsh entries, extracted with kaikki.org), available under CC BY-SA 4.0. About 4,000 more come from the Welsh–English data of the Apertium project (based on Eurfa by Kevin Donnelly and others), under the GNU GPL v2. Roughly a quarter of the words have a meaning; for the rest, tap the word and use the link to Geiriadur Prifysgol Cymru.

    Inspired by the book Yr Odliadur by Roy Stephens and Alan Llwyd (ISBN 978-1-84323-903-1). This is an independent tool, not affiliated with or endorsed by the authors or publisher. It does not reproduce the book, and its word list differs from the book's. The list of endings follows the chapter headings of the online copy of the book by Geraint Jones (Gwasg Aredig); the words under each ending come from the Bangor dictionary, not from the book. The y/wy rule is by spelling only, so a few words with the talgron wy sound (such as tanwydd, pinwydd) appear under -wy… rather than -y…. A list of words that share an ending is not the same as a list of words that rhyme in strict-metre poetry, so for the authoritative guide to rhyme, including the rules for wy, y, u and i, use the book.
